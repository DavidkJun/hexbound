HEXBOUND - STAGE 0 - TESTER

Goal of this stage: find the holes in the design before anyone builds on them, and decide how we will check later that the game plays the way it should. Budget is about 5 hours.


1. Review the design document for gaps

Read the whole design document and write down every place where two people could build the same rule in two different ways. For each point write: what is unclear, why it matters, and a suggested answer if you have one. Send the list to the PM.

Some questions to start with (not the full list):
- Health: does it carry over between fights and between levels? How do you heal besides villages and chests?
- Food: what happens when food runs out?
- Death: when you retry a level, do you restart with the deck, gold and health you had at the start of the level, or at the moment of death? Are slots dealt again?
- Map: can you go back to a tile you already visited? Does a cleared tile stay empty? Can you walk past a monster tile without fighting?
- Visibility: "details appear within 2 tiles" of the player only, or of the planned route too?
- Keys: does a key carry over to the next level? Is there always a locked chest on the same level as the elite?
- Combat: does block reset every turn? What happens to unplayed cards? Is there a hand size limit? When does the draw pile reshuffle?
- Keywords: exact meaning of Freeze, Chill, Exhaust and Draw. Is the ice hazard "chill" the same thing as the Chill keyword?
- Hazards: how much damage does lava deal, and when?
- Villages: what is a bounty? How much do rest, food and card removal cost?
- Mystery events: what dice, and how are odds shown?
- Score: how is it calculated?


2. Check the screen list

When the UI / level designer sends the screen list and sketches, check for missing screens and missing states: what the player sees when they cannot afford something, when the deck is empty, when a fight is won or lost, when there are no lives left.


3. Turn the pillars into a playtest checklist

For each design pillar, write 2-3 questions you can answer by watching someone play or by asking them afterwards. For example:
- The route is the main decision: did the player look at more than one route before moving? Did different routes lead to a noticeably different state at the boss?
- The map feeds the fight: did the player notice a hazard or a visited place changing the next fight?
- Few rules, real choices: on each screen, were there at least two options the player actually considered? Was any option always the right one?
- Growth you chose: by level 3, can the player say what their deck is built around?

Add the measurable targets from the design document, so playtests can be checked against numbers:
- A full run takes 35-45 minutes.
- A normal fight lasts 2-4 turns, a boss fight 6-10.
- Food on each level covers the direct route plus about 3 spare steps.
- Gold on each level pays for about two village purchases.
- No single route always wins.

Also make a simple table to fill in during playtests: player, level, route taken, food left, health at boss, fight lengths, run time, deaths, comments.


4. Agree with the programmer

- How you report bugs: the bug report template, where reports go, how severity is marked.
- How you know which build you are testing (version or commit shown somewhere in the game).
- Whether runs can be repeated with a seed, so bugs can be reproduced.


Hand off at the end of stage 0

- To the PM: the list of gaps and open questions.
- To the UI / level designer: missing screens and states, if any.
- To everyone: the playtest checklist and the playtest table.


Stage 0 is done for you when the PM has your gap list, the checklist exists, and you know how to report bugs.
